\section{Related Work and Novelty}

\subsection{LLMs for Formal Proof}

Neural theorem provers such as GPT-f~\cite{polu2020gptf},
LeanDojo~\cite{yang2023leandojo}, DeepSeek-Prover~\cite{xin2024deepseekproverv15,ren2025deepseekproverv2},
and AlphaProof~\cite{deepmind2024alphaproof} combine an LLM policy with a
sound verifier and reuse successful trajectories for training. We transfer
this closed-loop approach to FPV, where verifier runtime and discharged proof
obligations also provide graded feedback.

\subsection{LLMs for Hardware Verification}

Most LLM work in hardware targets RTL generation
\cite{liu2023verilogeval,liu2024rtlcoder}, assertion generation
\cite{fang2025assertllm,mali2024chiraag}, or testbench generation
\cite{qiu2024autobench}, rather than proving a given property. The closest
prior work is the lemma-mining framework of Peled et
al.~\cite{peled2026lemmaminers}, in which an LLM proposes invariants and a
model checker selects an inductive strengthening. Our work instead trains the
policy using supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning and targets
hierarchical assume-guarantee decomposition together with verified cutpoints,
abstractions, and blackboxing.

\subsection{Classical FPV Techniques}

Hardware model checking already uses localization abstraction
\cite{kurshan1994localization}, CEGAR~\cite{clarke2000cegar}, predicate and
proof-based abstraction~\cite{graf1997predicate,mcmillan2003proofbased}, and
word-level abstraction~\cite{goel2020avr,mann2021pono}. In industrial FPV,
engineers additionally create helper lemmas and module-level
assume-guarantee contracts. Our contribution is not a new proof rule, but an
agent that learns to apply these sound, established transformations in a
closed loop with the verifier.

\subsection{Data and Evaluation Gap}

Existing datasets are primarily generation-oriented: VERT
\cite{menon2025vert}, Verixa~\cite{verixa2025}, and NVIDIA CVDP
\cite{nvidiacvdp2025} provide SVA or verification examples but do not supply
verified hierarchical proof trajectories. Peled et
al.~\cite{peled2026lemmaminers} provide 109 flat SystemVerilog proof tasks.
This motivates our construction of design-level splits with verified
assume-guarantee examples and replayable proof trajectories
(Section~\ref{sec:finetuning}).
