\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate whether the trained agent proves more properties, and more
cheaply, than direct proving and prompting-based alternatives. All methods
run on the held-out test designs frozen during the downloadables phase
(Section~\ref{sec:implementation-plan}), under identical prover
configurations and wall-clock budgets.

\subsection{Test Cases}
\label{sec:exp-testcases}

The test set is the held-out portion of the verified corpus
(Section~\ref{sec:rl-instances}). Results are reported overall
and per difficulty tier (Section~\ref{sec:curriculum}); the hardest tier ---
instances the prover alone cannot discharge within budget --- measures
capability gain rather than speedup.

\subsection{Methods Compared}
\label{sec:exp-methods}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Prover-only.} SymbiYosys/Verific run directly on the
    target assertion with the full budget and no transformations; the
    baseline and the normalizer for relative proof time.
    \item \textbf{Frozen LLM with verifier feedback.} The pretrained base
    model in the environment loop, observing counterexamples, timeouts, and
    rejections over multiple steps --- the strongest training-free baseline.
    \item \textbf{Full agent.} The fully trained policy with the complete
    validated action space (lemmas, cutpoints, abstractions, blackboxing).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:exp-metrics}

\textbf{Effectiveness.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \emph{Solve rate}: fraction of instances whose target and full
    obligation closure are proved within budget, overall and per tier.
    \item \emph{Newly solved instances}: targets proved that prover-only
    cannot discharge within the same budget.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Efficiency} (on instances solved by both methods compared).
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \emph{End-to-end wall-clock time} to a completed sound proof;
    median and speedup relative to prover-only.
    \item \emph{Verifier time and calls}: cumulative prover CPU-time and
    obligations dispatched.
    \item \emph{Compute per solved target}: CPU- and GPU-hours per solve,
    making the LLM's own cost visible.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Proof and policy quality.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \emph{Proof-DAG size and depth}: helper properties and obligations
    in the accepted proof; smaller DAGs are more targeted and cheaper to
    re-check.
    \item \emph{Action validity rate}: fraction of actions accepted by the
    environment schema and design checks.
    \item \emph{Obligation discharge rate}: fraction of introduced
    obligations eventually proved, separating productive lemmas from
    speculative ones.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:exp-ablations}

On the validation split we ablate:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \emph{Action classes}: the lemma-only policy from the environment
    stage, and removing cutpoints, abstractions, and blackboxing individually
    from the full agent (mirroring their staged introduction during RL
    training).
    \item \emph{Reward shaping}: solve-only reward versus the full shaped
    reward of Section~\ref{sec:rl}.
    \item \emph{Curriculum}: the full difficulty mix from the start versus
    the graded curriculum of Section~\ref{sec:curriculum}.
    \item \emph{Fine-tuning stages}: the SFT-only model
    (Section~\ref{sec:finetuning}) without RL, and RL from the base model
    versus from each fine-tuning stage.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:exp-failures}

Unsolved instances are categorized by terminal state: prover timeout on a
valid decomposition, step-budget exhaustion without a viable DAG, persistent
invalid actions, or unproductive loops (repeated lemma--retract cycles).
